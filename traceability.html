Traceability

Adjectives are free. A mill name, a founding date, an hour count and a price broken into its parts are checkable — so those are what this page is made of.

Where the cloth is woven

The full mill record

How we choose them

We buy the bolt, not the metre, from a deliberately short list. That is what makes it possible to say which loom a given piece came off — and it is also why we run out.

Where the money goes

What a price is made of

Every product page breaks its own price into cloth, cutting, finishing, hardware, overhead and margin. Those breakdowns are not written per piece — they come from four construction profiles, and this is all four, so the figure on any product has something to be read against. They are grouped by how a thing is built rather than which line it is sold on — a made-to-order handbag still costs like a handbag.

The other half

What we cannot tell you yet

Everything above is checkable. These are the places where our record stops, written down because a traceability page with no gaps in it is not a traceability page — it is marketing, and you would be right to read it that way.

We trace cloth to the mill, not cotton to the field

We can tell you which loom wove your denim and, for most pieces, which weave run. We cannot yet tell you which farm grew the cotton, or under what conditions. Our mills can trace further back than we can publish; we are not going to claim their work as ours until we can show it.

We do not independently audit our mills

A mill's water figures are the mill's. We have visited all of them, we buy direct, and we believe them — but belief is not an audit and we will not print it as one.

The cost breakdowns are per piece at current volumes

They include duty into the EU and exclude returns, sampling, and the pieces we cut and rejected. A run of forty carries costs a run of four hundred does not, so these figures move as the house grows. They are honest, not precise.

We do not measure our carbon

Not by a method we would defend. Air freight, the atelier, the mills and your delivery all count, and we have not counted them. Buying less and keeping it longer is the honest claim we can make today, and it is what the repair promise is for.
